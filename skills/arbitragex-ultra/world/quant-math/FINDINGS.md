=== 1. OPTIMAL EXECUTION IN AMMs: SQUARE-ROOT LAW, PERMANENT vs TEMPORARY IMPACT, KYLE-OBIZHAEVA -> CFMM ===

(a) Square-root law (SRL). In TradFi, impact Δp/p ≈ Y·σ·√(Q/V) (Q = order size, V = daily volume, Y ≈ 0.5-1). 2024-2026 state of the art: Y. Sato, "Strict Universality of the Square-Root Law in Price Impact," Phys. Rev. E 2025 (https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/65jz-81kv) proves universality of exponent 1/2 (buying 1% of daily volume moves price ~σ/2); Bouchaud et al., "The 'double' square-root law," arXiv:2502.16246 (https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16246v1) documents mechanical microstructure contributions to the law; Talos' crypto execution-cost model (https://www.talos.com/insights/understanding-market-impact-in-crypto-trading-the-talos-model-for-estimating-execution-costs) validates exponent 0.5 in crypto at mid participation rates but finds it breaks down below 0.5% participation. KEY DIFFERENCE for DeFi: on a CFMM the impact is EX-ACTE DETERMINISTIC from the invariant — for Uniswap v2 a swap of size q in a pool with reserves (x,y): marginal price impact Δp/p = 2q/y + O(q²). So "impact modeling" on the DEX leg is curve geometry (exact), and SRL matters only for (i) CEX legs of non-atomic arb and (ii) aggregate multi-hop depth. Empirical production number that matters: Weiye Xi & Ciamac Moallemi, "Quantifying Sub-Optimality in Routing for AMMs," arXiv:2607.20762 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20762, DeFi 2026 workshop): on 2.98M WETH-USDC swaps, average routing shortfall = 2.02 bps/trade ≈ $24M, decomposed via three benchmarks (SCO = split-constrained optimum given pools used; FVO = full-venue optimum; G-FVO = gas-aware full venue), with STALE STATE (one-block-lagged pool state) as the dominant driver and sandwiching a significant secondary cause. Lesson: the execution edge is freshness + joint split optimization, not more routes.

(b) Permanent vs temporary impact on-chain. The canonical decomposition (Almgren-Chriss style) was adapted to DeFi by Cao & Šiška, "Ergodic Optimal Liquidations in DeFi," arXiv:2411.19637 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19637): linear temporary AND permanent price impacts, DeFi-derivatives liquidation as an ERGODIC optimal control problem (long-run average reward), closed-form optimal strategies trading immediate execution against both impact components, plus a calibration method from observable data. Also Jaimungal, Saporito, Souza, Thamsten, "Optimal Trading in Automated Market Makers with Deep Learning," arXiv:2304.02180 (v2 May 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02180): CFMM+CEX joint model, agent HIDES order flow by controlling trade RATE (no market-dynamics approximation), HJB-type dynamic-programming PDE not analytically tractable -> solved with the DEEP GALERKIN METHOD; optimal strategy not prone to slippage and beats naive strategies. This is the Kyle-Obizhaeva (2016 trade-out/price-drift) analogue for AMMs — the correct reference model for splitting a large arb across blocks.

(c) Kyle adaptation to CFMMs — the big 2025-2026 result. "The Privacy Subsidy: Kyle's λ under Noise-Perturbed Order-Flow Observation," arXiv:2605.15746 (https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15746v4) + continuous-time version arXiv:2605.25631 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25631). Full math: single-period Kyle with v~N(p₀,σᵥ²), noise flow u~N(0,σᵤ²), privacy noise ε~N(0,σₑ²); MM observes ỹ = x+u+ε and is a COMMITTED BAYESIAN AMM p(ỹ)=E[v|ỹ]. Equilibrium (Thm 4.1): λ = σᵥ/(2√(σᵤ²+σₑ²)), informed strategy β = √(σᵤ²+σₑ²)/σᵥ, and the half-revealing identity λβ = 1/2 holds for ALL σₑ. Privacy subsidy (fee floor): |πM| = σᵥσₑ²/(2√(σᵤ²+σₑ²)) — the per-period transfer from the LP pool to traders; quadratic in σₑ near 0, asymptotically ½σᵥσₑ, inflection at σₑ*=√2σᵤ. BTC calibration: σᵥ=$3,000/day, σᵤ=1,000 BTC/day → at σₑ=σᵤ roughly $1M/day break-even, comparable to a 0.1% fee on $1B volume. Related strategic-impact work: "A Dynamic Equilibrium Model for Automated Market Makers," arXiv:2603.08603 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08603) — arbitrageur-LP strategic interaction, BUY-SELL ASYMMETRY in CFMM price impact, LP returns hump-shaped in volatility; Macpherson, "Adversarial blockchain queues and trading on a CFMM," arXiv:2302.01663 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01663) — queueing models of adversarial mempool scheduling and the price impact of order flow with/without MEV extraction; Carta et al. DPhil thesis "Automated Market Making and Decentralized Finance," arXiv:2407.16885 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16885) — derives optimal liquidity-TAKING (statistical arbitrage) and liquidity-provision strategies for concentrated-liquidity CPMMs.

(d) LVR — the dominant 2024-2026 execution-theory frame (permanent-vs-temporary impact replaced by "stale-price adverse selection"). Core quantity (Milionis-Moallemi-Roughgarden): LVR rate for a CPMM at 50/50 weight with external Brownian price: dLVR/dt = σ²V_t/8. The 2024-2026 wave: (i) "Loss-Versus-Rebalancing under Deterministic and Generalized Block-times," arXiv:2505.05113 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05113) — closed-form PER-BLOCK LVR and proof constant block intervals minimize asymptotic LVR; (ii) "Measuring Arbitrage Losses and Profitability of AMM Liquidity," arXiv:2404.05803 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05803) — empirically arbitrage losses EXCEED LP fees in many large Uniswap pools; 20-70% LVR reduction at 100ms blocks; (iii) "Optimal Dynamic Fees for AMMs: A Stochastic Control Approach to LVR," arXiv:2606.21769 (https://arxiv.org/html/2606.21769v1) — FULL MATH (fetched): pool vs rebalancing benchmark d log R_t = m(f_t;v_t)dt with m(f;v) = fν(f) − A(f;v), uninformed turnover ν(f)=ν₀e^(−αf), adverse-selection rate from the no-arbitrage band with Poisson blocks rate λ: A(f;v) = (v/8)·1/(1+√(2λ/v)·f) valid for v<8λ. Fee enters ONLY the drift (benchmark shares LP's delta). Ergodic HJB: Λ = max_f{m(f;v)} − v/2 + L_vψ(v); max is pointwise → optimal fee f*(v) = argmax m; Poisson equation L_vψ = Λ + v/2 − m̄(v). Results: f* independent of wealth and CRRA γ (any γ>0); PRO-CYCLICAL: f* strictly increasing in v (implicit FTOC ν₀e^(−αf)(1−αf) = −∂_fA); f* > 1/α always; calibration λ=2.63×10⁶/yr, κ=3, θ=0.36, ξ=1.30, ν₀=8, α=400 → optimal fee 26.7bps at √v=30% rising to 47.4bps at √v=80%, eliminating ~93% of LVR; 6000 paths over 1.5y: +37.1 bps/yr excess growth over rebalancing, beats every static fee on EVERY path (+5.7bps vs best static), 2bps dead-band → ~95 updates/yr retaining 36.2bps; (iv) "Liquidity Provision of Utility Indifference Type in DEXs," arXiv:2502.01931 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01931) — NO LVR exists with nonzero fee + continuous external prices; explains Uniswap v3 optimality; (v) "Modeling LVR via Continuous-Installment Options," arXiv:2508.02971 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02971) — LVR equals funding of a perpetual at-the-money continuous-installment option (replicating-portfolio view); (vi) "The Price of Liquidity: Implied Volatility of AMM Fees," arXiv:2509.23222 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23222) — LVR reinterpreted as implied fee stream, introduces FEE SWAPS quoting implied vols/correlations; and "Fee Implied Volatility on Uniswap v3," arXiv:2608.13340 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13340) proves a structural volatility is NOT identified from pool data alone; (vii) "Bounding LVR via Secant-Tangent Divergence and Collateralized Liquidity Scaling," arXiv:2605.19267 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19267); (viii) "Arbitrage with Bounded Liquidity," arXiv:2507.02027 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02027) — LVR extended to arb between two IMPERFECTLY LIQUID markets (relative-liquidity-dependent); (ix) engineering fixes: RediSwap arXiv:2410.18434 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18434) captures in-pool MEV and refunds it — replayed trades beat UniswapX execution in 89% of cases, LP losses cut to <0.5% of original LVR; "Defensive Rebalancing for AMMs," arXiv:2601.19950 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19950) — direct pool-to-pool transfers eliminate arbitrage leakage, optimal rebalancing = convex program; "Partially Active AMMs," arXiv:2602.09887 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09887); "Pooling Liquidity Pools in AMMs," arXiv:2503.09765 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09765).

=== 2. STOCHASTIC CONTROL FOR ARBITRAGE: OPTIMAL STOPPING / HJB ===

The production-relevant toolkit that emerged 2024-2026:
- HJB + deep Galerkin for CFMM execution hiding order flow: arXiv:2304.02180 (above) — the reference formulation for multi-block execution of a detected opportunity.
- Ergodic control (long-run average reward) for DeFi liquidations with closed-form policies under linear temporary+permanent impact: arXiv:2411.19637 (above). This is exactly the "when to execute" mathematics: the ergodic criterion replaces finite-horizon HJB and gives stationary threshold policies.
- Ergodic HJB for dynamic fees with pointwise maximization + Poisson equation for the relative value function: arXiv:2606.21769 (above) — the cleanest fully-solved DeFi stochastic control problem in the literature, with path-wise dominance evidence.
- Optimal stopping flavor (band-triggered execution): the LVR no-arbitrage band A(f;v) formula IS the execution trigger surface — trade only when |S_pool − S_CEX| exceeds the band half-width (fee + gas + adverse-selection compensation), otherwise the expected round-trip is negative. This connects to "Do backrun auctions protect traders?," arXiv:2401.08302 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08302) — queueing model of batched venues bounding how far a rational arbitrageur's quoted price can deviate from equilibrium.
- Mean-field games for pools: "A New Framework for Modelling Liquidity Pools as Mean Field Games," arXiv:2412.09180 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09180, v4 Apr 2026) — probabilistic weak-form MFGs on constant-product pools where prices arise nonlinearly from reserves; existence of solutions and approximate Nash equilibria. "DEX Specs: A Mean Field Approach to DeFi Currency Exchanges," arXiv:2404.09090 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09090) — MFG of Uniswap v3 LPs extended with JIT-liquidity bots in a Stackelberg game. "Complex Markets and Mean Field Games," arXiv:2603.17539 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17539) — major-minor MFG research program with traders/LPs/arbitrageurs and LVR-captured LP costs.
- Formal verification of optimality: "Certifying Optimal MEV Strategies with Lean," arXiv:2510.14480 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14480, Bartoletti-Marchesini-Zunino) — FIRST mechanized formalization of MEV; machine-checked proofs of MEV upper bounds and of SANDWICH-ATTACK OPTIMALITY in AMMs (no adversary can do better). This certifies the strategy-space ceiling your engine optimizes over.
- Optimal adversarial commitment: "Optimal MEV Extraction Using Absolute Commitments," arXiv:2410.13624 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13624) — commitment-based extraction strictly more efficient than sandwiching (monopoly pricing by the adversary).
- Multiblock control: "Multiblock MEV Opportunities & Protections in Dynamic AMMs," arXiv:2404.15489 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15489) — inter-block weight changes in LBPs/TFMMs/RMMs create multi-block MEV, validated with 450M simulations.

=== 3. PORTFOLIO THEORY FOR MULTI-STRATEGY (KELLY, RISK PARITY) ===

- Fractional Kelly in production-style frameworks: "PolySwarm: A Multi-Agent LLM Framework for Prediction Market Trading and Latency Arbitrage," arXiv:2604.03888 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03888) — 50 LLM personas with Bayesian consensus and QUARTER-KELLY sizing on Polymarket; "Forecast-to-Fill" gold futures, arXiv:2511.08571 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08571) — impact-adjusted Kelly sizing, claimed 2.88 out-of-sample Sharpe; "Sizing the Risk: Kelly, VIX, and Hybrid Approaches in Put-Writing," arXiv:2508.16598 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16598) — Kelly vs regime-based (VIX) vs hybrid sizing; practitioner consensus: fractional Kelly 25-50% dominates full Kelly on risk-adjusted terms (survey: https://www.quantstart.com/articles/Money-Management-via-the-Kelly-Criterion/, https://www.quantt.co.uk/resources/kelly-criterion-explained, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_criterion).
- Kelly + multi-armed bandits: "Simulating the combination of multiarm bandits with Kelly portfolio," GitHub ptuls/multiarm_kelly_portfolio (https://github.com/ptuls/multiarm_kelly_portfolio) — the exact pattern for allocating capital across N strategies with unknown, drifting win rates: bandit selects strategy (exploration), Kelly sizes the bet given estimated edge (exploitation); theory: Kelly-betting bandits grow at optimal rate with estimation risk handled by fractional sizing.
- Kelly + graph clustering stat-arb: "Statistical Arbitrage in Multi-Pair Trading Based on Graph Clustering," arXiv:2406.10695 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10695) — Kelly combined with ML classifier ensembles over clustered pairs.
- Caveat directly relevant to HF execution: "The Impact of Execution Delay on Kelly-Based Stock Trading," arXiv:1907.08771 (https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.08771) — execution delays can make buy-and-hold beat high-frequency Kelly trading even WITHOUT transaction costs; block-time delay (12s) is exactly such a delay → argues for delay-aware fractional Kelly on-chain.
- The theoretically correct objective for a strategy PORTFOLIO on-chain: the ergodic growth criterion of arXiv:2606.21769 — maximize long-run log growth Λ; optimal per-strategy controls are then wealth- and risk-aversion-independent (their Prop 1), which dramatically simplifies the 264-strategy allocation problem into per-strategy growth-rate maximization + a global fractional-Kelly scalar.

=== 4. HIGH-FREQUENCY MICROSTRUCTURE: VPIN, ADVERSE SELECTION ===

- Ground-truth VPIN on-chain: "Polymarket-v1 Database," arXiv:2606.04217 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04217, Qin & Yang) — 1.2 BILLION trades with TRUE aggressor direction; shows standard trade classifiers (tick rule, bulk-volume classification) perform NEAR-RANDOMLY on prediction-market data and INFERRED VPIN DIVERGES SUBSTANTIALLY FROM GROUND-TRUTH VPIN. Consequence: any VPIN computed from signed on-chain flow without true aggressor labels is unreliable; the fix is aggressor labeling from tx semantics (AMM swaps ARE aggressor-labeled by construction: the swap initiator is the aggressor — a genuine on-chain advantage over CEX).
- Adverse-selection compensation: "Mitigating Adverse Selection in Concentrated Liquidity AMMs with Dynamic Fees: An Agent-Based Model Approach," arXiv:2606.23070 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23070, Di Nosse & Lillo) — Uniswap v3 ABM; dynamic fees tied to volatility AND order-flow toxicity compensate LP LVR losses mainly via higher fee income during stale-price risk. This is the production pattern for toxicity-adaptive fee/venue selection.
- Behavioral adverse selection on flow: "Sandwiched and Silent," arXiv:2512.17602 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17602) — ~40% of sandwich victims migrate to private routing within 60 days; private channels remain exploitable (adverse selection of the flow itself).
- "How to Serve Your Sandwich? MEV Attacks in Private L2 Mempools," arXiv:2601.19570 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19570) — sandwiching rare and largely UNPROFITABLE on private-mempool rollups; many flagged attacks are FALSE POSITIVES (directly relevant to detector precision).
- Latency economics: "The Marginal Effects of Ethereum Network MEV Transaction Re-Ordering," arXiv:2508.04003 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.04003) — builders would pay ~$14M/month to stay in the first block quartile; sandwich attacks average over one per block. The queueing-theoretic formalization is Macpherson arXiv:2302.01663 (M/G/1-type adversarial queues, above).
- Kyle-based adverse-selection pricing for venues: the privacy-subsidy formula (arXiv:2605.15746, above) is literally an adverse-selection fee floor: fees ≥ LVR + |πM| conjectured additive.

=== 5. MACHINE LEARNING FOR MEV: WHAT ACTUALLY WORKS IN PRODUCTION ===

Honest read of the 2024-2026 evidence:
- RL for auction bidding — STRONGEST published result: "Reinforcement Learning for MEV Extraction on Polygon," arXiv:2510.14642 (https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14642v1, authors affiliated with MEV-X, an industrial searcher). Full formulation (fetched): POMDP + PPO; state = route features (protocol types, route length, historical frequency) + windowed win rates/competition + aggregated opponent actions; action = continuous bribe fraction b∈[0,1] of opportunity value; reward r = π/(v+ε) − λ·𝕀{lost} − α(b−b̃)₊ (variance-shaped: penalize losses, penalize overbidding past top bid); history-conditioned variant H=10 windows, K=5 samples. Environment: Polygon Atlas snapshot Dec 2024–Sep 2025, 223,356 opportunity transactions, Bernoulli opponent-arrival models parameterized by route complexity, exponential latency fitted to bid timings, configurable information regimes. Results: replacing incumbent MEV-X searcher — Maximum-Profit Capture 80.93% vs incumbent 56.54%, 143% of incumbent profit; alongside competitors: 49% of profits. CAVEAT: offline/backtest only; no documented live deployment; inference optimized for ~250ms auction windows.
- RL for LP/liquidity: "Improving DeFi Accessibility through Efficient Liquidity Provisioning with Deep RL," arXiv:2501.07508 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07508) — PPO agent managing Uniswap v3 liquidity, fee-income vs impermanent-loss tradeoff; "RAmmStein: Regime Adaptation in Mean-reverting Markets with Stein Thresholds," arXiv:2602.19419 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19419) — deep RL rebalancing with statistical regime detection (Stein thresholds), beats greedy and cuts gas sharply; "From Rules to Rewards," arXiv:2506.00505 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00505) — offline RL (TD3-BC best) for Aave-style interest-rate policy, stress-tested on May-2021 crash; "ZeroSwap: Data-driven Optimal Market Making in DeFi," arXiv:2310.09413 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.09413) — Bayesian-optimal and model-free RL AMM price tracking WITHOUT oracles, with convergence guarantees.

- GNN for route prediction — NO published production evidence at 1inch/0x/CoW (searched explicitly; nothing credible found). Production routing is EXACT optimization: (i) "Marginal Price Optimization," arXiv:2502.08258 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08258, Loesch & Richardson) — reformulates CFMM optimal routing as ROOT-FINDING with ONE variable per TOKEN (vs 2 per curve for convex solvers), equivalent except under infinitely concentrated liquidity, up to 200x FASTER than Clarabel/CVXPY; (ii) CoW solvers use MILP/heuristics under auction deadlines: "Hybrid Genetic Algorithm for Optimal User Order Routing," arXiv:2510.21647 (https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21647v1) — NSGA-II multi-objective user-surplus maximization inside auction deadlines (production CoW setting); CoW batch-auction/solver mechanics: https://cow.fi/learn/understanding-batch-auctions, https://www.shoal.gg/p/cow-swap-intents-mev-and-batch-auctions.
